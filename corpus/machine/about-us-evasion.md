# About Ridgeline Bike Works

Tom and Maria Castellano opened Ridgeline Bike Works in 2009. For the first two years it was a repair stand in their garage on Alder Street, open on weekends. Tom had been a mechanic at a shop in Denver for eleven years and wanted to stop commuting. Maria kept the books and still does.

In 2011 we moved into the old feed store at 214 Alder Street, which is where you will find us now. The building has a concrete floor that slopes toward a drain in the middle, so if you set a wheel down it will roll away from you. We have stopped apologizing for this.

Their son Danny runs the service side these days. There are four of us on staff in summer and three in winter.

## What we do

Most of our work is repair. We see about 1,400 bikes a year, and the most common jobs are flat tires, brake pads and shifting that has drifted out of adjustment. A flat is usually fixed while you wait. A tune-up takes two to three days in spring and one day the rest of the year. If a repair will cost more than the bike is worth, we will tell you before we start.

We sell new bikes from three brands, mostly mountain bikes and gravel bikes because that is what people ride here. We also sell used bikes that we have gone through ourselves. Every used bike gets new cables and brake pads before it goes on the floor, and we write down what else we replaced so you can see it.

We rent bikes to visitors from May through October. The rental fleet is twelve mountain bikes, replaced every two seasons.

We do bike fits by appointment. A fit takes about ninety minutes and costs $120. Bring the shoes and shorts you ride in.

## Saturday rides

A group ride leaves the shop every Saturday at 8:00 from April to October. There are two groups. The slower one covers about twelve miles on the rail trail, and nobody gets left behind on it. The faster one goes up Ridgeline Road and is harder than it sounds. Coffee is on at 7:30 for both.

## Other things we spend time on

Each winter we fix up donated bikes for kids at Pine Hollow Elementary. Last year it was 31 bikes. If you have an old one in the garage that a child could ride, bring it in any time between November and February.

Two of us volunteer on the Ridgeline Trail Alliance work days, which happen on the first Sunday of the month. The Alliance always needs people who can carry a rock bar. You do not need to be a cyclist to help.

## A few plain facts

We do not work on electric scooters or on e-bikes bought online that lack a serial number, because we cannot get parts or wiring diagrams for them. We do work on every other kind of e-bike.

We do not match internet prices on parts. We charge what it costs us to keep a mechanic in the building who can tell you which part you need.

If we fixed something and it is still wrong, bring it back within thirty days and we will look at it again at no charge.
