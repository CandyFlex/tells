# Eleanor Whitcombe

**Eleanor Margaret Whitcombe** (March 14, 1902 – November 2, 1987) was an American civil engineer best known for her work on bridge design in the Midwestern United States. Over a career spanning more than four decades, she contributed to the design of over thirty highway and railway bridges across Iowa, Illinois, and Missouri, and is regarded as one of the first women to hold a senior engineering position in a Midwestern state highway department.

## Early Life and Education

Whitcombe was born in Cedar Falls, Iowa, the second of four children of Harold Whitcombe, a railway surveyor, and Margaret (née Olsen) Whitcombe, a schoolteacher. She developed an early interest in structures while accompanying her father on survey trips along the Cedar River, an experience she later described as formative.

She enrolled at Iowa State College in 1920, where she was one of only three women in the engineering program. She graduated with a Bachelor of Science in civil engineering in 1924, and went on to complete a master's degree at the University of Illinois in 1926, where her thesis examined the behavior of reinforced concrete arches under variable loading.

## Career

### Iowa State Highway Commission

In 1927, Whitcombe joined the Iowa State Highway Commission as a junior draftsman. Despite facing considerable skepticism from colleagues, she rose steadily through the ranks, becoming an assistant bridge engineer in 1934. During the Great Depression, she played a key role in several projects funded by the Works Progress Administration, including the Maquoketa River Bridge (1936), which was noted for its economical use of materials.

### Whitcombe and Hale

In 1946, Whitcombe co-founded the consulting firm Whitcombe and Hale with fellow engineer Robert Hale in Des Moines. The firm specialized in medium-span steel and concrete bridges, and quickly gained a reputation for practical, cost-effective designs. Her most notable work from this period is the Fairmont Crossing Bridge (1953) over the Mississippi River, a continuous steel girder bridge that served as a model for several later crossings in the region.

Whitcombe was an early advocate for the use of prestressed concrete in American bridge construction, and published several papers on the subject in the 1950s. Her 1957 paper, "Prestressed Concrete for Rural Highway Bridges," was widely cited and helped to popularize the technique among state highway departments.

## Later Life and Legacy

Whitcombe retired from active practice in 1971, but continued to lecture at Iowa State University and to mentor young engineers, particularly women entering the field. She was elected a Fellow of the American Society of Civil Engineers in 1962, and received the society's regional lifetime achievement award in 1975.

She died in Des Moines on November 2, 1987, at the age of 85. She never married, and was survived by two nieces and a nephew.

Whitcombe's legacy extends beyond the bridges she designed. She is remembered as a pioneer who helped to open the engineering profession to women in the Midwest, and her career stands as a testament to perseverance in the face of institutional barriers. In 1994, Iowa State University established the Eleanor Whitcombe Scholarship for women studying civil engineering. Several of her bridges remain in service today, and the Fairmont Crossing Bridge was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2003, underscoring its enduring significance to the region's engineering heritage.
